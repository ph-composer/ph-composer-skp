## 2. Size

`tools/map_data/map_borders.py` simplifies each outer ring by Douglas-Peucker on degrees (countries
0.08°, United States and Canada states and provinces 0.02°), rounds to 0.01°, drops rings under
0.05 square degrees, and writes the changes from point to point in the encoded polyline algorithm.
The United States and Canada are simplified as finely as their states and provinces (0.02°), so the solid
country outline and the dashed state lines drawn over it agree along the coast, and their land is filled
like every other country's. Result: 649 country rings and 178 state and province rings, 33,608 points.

| | Bytes |
| --- | --- |
| `html/map_borders.js`, the generated module | 113,186 |
| The first draft's data (no United States or Canada outline) as decimal integer arrays | 186,589 |
| The same draft in the encoded polyline algorithm | 90,352 |
| Everything this stage adds to the installed extension (the module, `map_projection.js`, `outline_map.js` and `.css`, Set Location's growth, the runtime manifest) | 150,130 |
| Budget, uncompressed | 300,000 |

The RBZ before and after, on `make rbz` (same machine, 2026-11-18): 8,339,416 bytes compressed and
25,710,856 bytes installed before, at the stage's base `d38dfbf2`; 8,422,288 and 25,860,986 after, 82,872
and 150,130 more. The runtime manifest's installed ceiling is 26,000,000 bytes, so 139,014 remain for
the rest of the packet; the first decimal draft would have left 69,963, which is why the encoding changed.

Detail at the closest zoom, a 0.5° tall view: state lines are within about 2 km of the source and
read as the states they are; coastlines of countries other than the United States and Canada are
coarser (0.08°, about 9 km). The browser evidence screenshots show both.

## 3. Chromium

The development example (`tools/examples/outline_map/`, `?example=outline_map&markers=1000`) in the
browser evidence tool (`tools/ui-preview/outline_map_checks.cjs`), headless Chrome 157 on this Mac,
a 900 by 600 px map with 1,000 markers, a ring and the borders:

| Measure | Result |
| --- | --- |
| First paint, cold (the page's first map builds the border paths) | 9 to 12 ms over seven runs |
| First paint, warm (a second map, 1,000 markers) | 3 to 10 ms |
| Pan, 60 pointer moves, frame interval | mean 16.7 ms, p95 17.5 to 19.0 ms, max 18.4 to 21.4 ms |
| Pan, script time per move | mean 2.1 to 2.6 ms, p95 at most 2.9 ms |
| Wheel zoom, 30 steps, frame interval | mean 16.7 ms, p95 16.8 to 18.8 ms, max 17.7 to 25.6 ms |
| Wheel zoom, script time per step | mean 2.0 to 2.5 ms, p95 at most 3.0 ms |

The last runs are on the code after the review fixes, which paint a pan or wheel step once per display frame,
with the United States and Canada outlines in the data.

Every frame landed on the display's 60 Hz interval. The borders are one path per layer under one SVG
transform with `vector-effect: non-scaling-stroke`, so a pan or zoom rewrites the transform and the
markers, not the borders.

The floor: ESLint's API list passes on the new files with no new exception, and they use nothing the
list refuses (`replaceChildren` and `ResizeObserver` are older than Chromium 88). First paint and frame
time in the real HtmlDialog are CM07's host run.

## 4. Content policy

The data is a `PhComposerUI.define` module among the panel's own scripts, listed in `index.html` by
`make panel-manifest` and pinned by the runtime manifest. `tests/page_csp_test.js` passes unchanged.
The browser check opens the shipped `html/index.html` under its own policy, opens Set Location with an
attached station, pans, zooms and resets: no request after the panel loaded, every request under the
extension's `html/`, and no `securitypolicyviolation` event.

## 5. C4c-5: projection and zoom range

Equirectangular, with longitude scaled by the cosine of the view's centre latitude, so a ring at the
Phius distance limit near the centre of the view draws as a circle (radii within 2% in
`tests/map_projection_test.js`). The ring itself is the great-circle locus on the limit check's sphere,
so it is exact whatever the projection does. The view's centre is held within 80° of the equator. The
zoom range is the latitude span shown top to bottom: 170° (the whole world) to 0.5° (about 55 km).
Recorded as D4c-26.
