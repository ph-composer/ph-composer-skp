The entry check of [P4c-11](../phases/p4c-11-hand-entry-and-edit-values.md). Shape only: rows,
columns, separators, where the labels and blanks sit, and the order of the series. No station name,
label text, value or file name from the licensed corpus is here. The corpus is the one
`_private/climate/MANIFEST.md` locates, read in place by two scratch scripts outside the repository
that printed type codes (number, text, blank) and counts only.

## The workbook

- The sheet holds two blocks of the same shape, one above the other: the first in SI, the second
  in IP. Each block is two heading rows (the month numbers and the days in each month), one row of
  station facts, then the eight series rows.
- A series row is one label cell, then twelve monthly cells, then three design cells. In both
  blocks the design columns are headed Heating 1, Heating 2 and Cooling, in that order.
- The first six series rows (outdoor temperature, then the five radiation rows north, east, south,
  west and global) hold fifteen numbers. The last two (dewpoint, sky temperature) hold twelve
  numbers and three blank cells.
- The series order is outdoor temperature, north, east, south, west, global, dewpoint, sky
  temperature in every workbook, the PRD's order, which the grid uses.
- The IP block's radiation is in kBtu/ft², the panel's IP unit for monthly radiation.

## The station text file

- Tab-separated, CRLF line ends, Windows-1252. The eight series lines follow one header line, in
  the same order as the workbook.
- Each series line is one label cell, then twelve monthly cells and three design cells. The first
  six lines hold fifteen numbers; the dewpoint and sky lines hold twelve numbers and three short
  text notes where the design values would be.

## 3. A row of twelve

General: in every workbook and every station file the dewpoint and sky rows carry twelve values
and no design numbers. Pasted, they fill their months and leave their design cells empty. Those
six cells are optional under D4c-08 (a case's dewpoint and sky temperature may be null), so such a
paste does **not** save as Incomplete, as the stage document had it: a full paste of the eight rows
is complete once the name, station elevation, daily swing and wind speed are typed. From a station
file the three notes on each of those two lines are not numbers, so the paste notice counts six
values left empty.

## 4. One layout, with a label column left out

Both sources put a label cell before the values on every series row, so a copy of whole lines from
a station file, or of the workbook's label column with its values, is sixteen cells wide and would
not fit the fifteen-column grid. The paste therefore leaves out a leading column when every row's
first cell is text that is not a number; anything else pastes as copied. One accepted layout is
enough beyond that: the eight series rows, twelve months and three design columns, in the file's
order. A copy that takes the workbook's two heading rows as well is ten rows tall and is refused
whole. Recorded as D4c-34 in [DECISIONS](../DECISIONS.md).

## 1 and 2. The clipboard, not measured here

What Excel and a text editor actually put on the clipboard (separators, line ends, a trailing line
end, blank cells as empty strings, quoting) needs the real applications in front, which this stage
does not take. The grid reads the clipboard's `text/plain` with the shared tab-separated parser,
which takes tab, CR, LF and CRLF and a trailing line end. CM11 part B pastes a real block from each
and reads the grid back ([recipe](cm11-recipe.md)); nothing from the file is kept.
